NodeAva Workshop

Lab

localhost:3000/lab/

Build a digital human
one organ at a time.

Six self-contained labs. Each brings up one component, lets you poke it directly, and shows the latency it costs. By the end you've assembled a complete digital human on your own laptop — and you know what every layer is doing.

Lab 1 · ~20 min

The Brain — local LLM

Ollama on the host. Stream tokens from qwen3:4b-instruct, watch time-to-first-token, measure tokens-per-second. Toggle the lab's Backend dropdown to test LM Studio (native API) instead. This is the cognition layer; nothing else works until it does.
Lab 2 · ~15 min

The Ears — speech-to-text

Whisper running in Docker with GPU acceleration. Record from your mic, see the transcript come back, learn why endpointing (knowing when the user is done) matters more than transcription accuracy.
Lab 3 · ~15 min

The Voice — text-to-speech

Kokoro neural TTS. Type a phrase, hear it spoken with word-level timestamps. Compare voices. Learn what first-audio latency and real-time factor mean for "feels alive vs feels dead".
Lab 4 · ~20 min

The Nervous System — orchestrator

The FastAPI service that holds the personality system prompt and routes every request. Swap personalities live. See how the same model gives radically different answers based on the prompt.
Lab 5 · ~25 min

The Hands — agentic tools

The agentic loop. Wiki recall (RAG over local docs) and live web search. Watch the model emit tool_calls, see the round-by-round event stream, learn why small models stumble on tool use.
Lab 6 · ~25 min

The Whole Body — full pipeline

Mic → STT → LLM → TTS → avatar. End-to-end latency budget. Why streaming + sentence chunking is the difference between "feels alive" and "wait, is it broken?"
